# ADR 0024: Voiding a Loan Whose Payout Came Back, and Returning Reserve Cover the Loan Could Not Take

## Status
Accepted. Closes two risks listed in [ADR 0017](0017-savings-and-loan-money-through-paypal.md) ("a payout returned after the loan was registered is flagged for a person, not unwound") and [ADR 0020](0020-member-reads-verified-actors-and-reserve-cover-on-the-schedule.md) ("when `unusedCents` is not zero, the ledger holds a credit that the schedule never took"). Builds on [ADR 0002](0002-ledger-transaction-boundary.md) (a posting commits with the state it records), [ADR 0004](0004-repayment-schedule-immutable-versions.md) (append-only facts) and [ADR 0019](0019-reserve-safety-fund-and-fraud-signals.md) (the fund).

## Context
**A payout that comes back after the loan exists.** A loan is registered when the payout succeeds (ADR 0017). PayPal can still take a delivered payout back (a reversal, a recipient account closed). Until now lending only set `payoutIssue = RETURNED_AFTER_DISBURSEMENT` and left a live loan: a schedule, autopay, reminders, a receivable of the full principal, a record that counted a loan and, thirty days later, a missed instalment, all for money the member never kept.

**A cover the loan could not take.** Reserve posts a cover to the ledger (debit `LOSS_RESERVE`, credit `LOANS_RECEIVABLE`) when the second officer signs; lending applies it to the schedule when it hears `CoverApproved`. If the member pays between the two, lending takes only what is still owed and reports the rest as `unusedCents`, but the ledger had credited all of it: the loan's receivable went below what the schedule says and the fund was short by the difference. Nothing corrected it.

## Decision

### 1. A returned disbursement voids the loan
The design rule: *the member never received the money, so the loan must not stay live and the member owes nothing.* `DisbursementReturn.unwind` runs inside `MicroloanApplicationService.onPayoutReturned` for an application that is `DISBURSED` and whose **current** payout reference came back, in that transaction, with the application row and then the loan row locked:

1. **Ledger: one new entry**, source key `loan-void:<loanId>`. It reverses the disbursement and settles who owns what the loan had received. With `P` the principal, `O` what the schedule still owed, `c` what the loss reserve paid (the `RESERVE` receipts) and `m = P - O - c` what the member paid:

   | Side | Account | Amount |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | debit | `PROGRAM_CASH` | `P` (the money is back in the program account) |
   | credit | `LOANS_RECEIVABLE(loan)` | `O` (the receivable goes to zero) |
   | credit | `MEMBER_OVERPAYMENT(member)` | `m` (the member's own money is held for them) |
   | credit | `LOSS_RESERVE` | `c` (the fund's money goes back to the fund) |

   Debits equal credits because `P = O + m + c`. The disbursement entry, the repayments and every other fact stay as they are.
2. **The loan** is closed with a new terminal status `DISBURSEMENT_RETURNED` (migration `lending/V7`): outstanding 0, `overpaid_cents = held overpayment + m`, `closed_on` today. Its unfired timers are deleted and planned retries cancelled; it is no longer `ACTIVE`, so close of business never selects it (no instalment can be missed, no bucket moves), `collectionSpec` is not collectible (the mandate sync that follows cancels autopay), no reminder timer exists, and `MemberViews` does not list it. `LoanRecalculator.recalculate` is a no-op for it, and `integrity` checks it against the ledger without refolding the schedule.
3. **The application** returns to `APPROVED` with `payoutIssue = RETURNED_AFTER_DISBURSEMENT`, `loanId`, `disbursedAt` and the cooling-off window cleared, and `disbursement_voids + 1` (`lending/V7`). An officer can **disburse again** after fixing the recipient: the payout reference is already new (`<id>-a<n>`, ADR 0017), and the loan reference becomes `loan-<id>-disb-v<voids>` because the first one is spent on the voided loan (`lending.loan.disbursement_ref` is unique and registration is idempotent on it, so reusing it would hand back the void loan). Or the officer can **reject** it: `reject` is now allowed on an `APPROVED` application whose `payoutIssue` is set (a plain approved application is still not rejectable). `LoanApplicationChanged` announces the new state.
4. **Governance audit**: `LOAN_DISBURSEMENT_RETURNED`, subject the loan, actor `SYSTEM/lending`, with the application, payout reference, a sanitised reason, principal, refund due and reserve cover.
5. **Event**: `LendingEvents.LoanVoided(loanId, memberId, applicationId, payoutReference, reason, principalCents, refundDueCents, reserveCoverCents)` and `MandateSyncRequested`.

**Idempotent on the payout reference.** The application leaves `DISBURSED` in the same transaction, so a replayed `PayoutReturned` finds no disbursed application and changes nothing; `currentPayout` already drops a report whose reference is not the application's current one, so a late report about attempt 1 cannot void the loan of attempt 2. The ledger entry is keyed by the loan and the audit and event exist once per void.

**Edge cases decided.**
- *Return before registration finished.* While `DISBURSING` the return puts the application back to `APPROVED` (as before; no loan exists). A delayed `PayoutSucceeded` that follows is ignored because the application is no longer disbursing. The row lock on the application serialises the two handlers whichever commits first.
- *Return of a withdrawal payout.* A savings withdrawal's reference is not an application id (or names no application), so `currentPayout` finds nothing and savings handles it; lending touches nothing.
- *The member already repaid before the return.* **Decision: refund in code, never keep.** The member's payments become held overpayment (`m` above, plus any overpayment already held), and after the commit `CollectionEffects` asks PayPal for the refund of `refundDueCents` with the key `void-refund:<loanId>`, the same path as the automatic refund of an overpaid loan (a PayPal outage retries the event; amounts the captures cannot cover are logged for the desk and the money stays held and visible on the loan). The refund completes through the existing `OverpaymentRefundCompleted` path, which for a void loan books `debit MEMBER_OVERPAYMENT, credit PROGRAM_CASH` and lowers the held amount. The record is not told of the payments as repayments, and the void loan reports no payments. A payment that reaches a void loan later (an autopay charge or checkout in flight) is held and refunded the same way (`VoidedLoanPaymentHeld`), a reversal of a payment on a void loan lowers the held amount, and a reserve cover that arrives for it is `NOTHING_DUE` and goes back to the fund (section 2).
- *Reserve cover on the loan.* The reserve's share `c` goes back to `LOSS_RESERVE` in the same entry, and `reserve` listens to `LoanVoided` to bring the fund counters, its covers (`returned_cents`) and its open exposure in line (`VoidedLoanListener`, idempotent on `loan-void:<loanId>`).

### 2. Cover the loan could not take goes back to the fund
`CoverRepaymentAdapter` (the `app` wiring) now reads `CoverResult.unusedCents`. If it is positive it calls the new `ReserveFacade.returnUnusedCover(approvalRef, unusedCents, "LOAN_OWED_LESS")` in the same transaction as the application of the rest (`MANDATORY`), so the schedule, the ledger and the fund move together or not at all. Reserve (`CoverReturns`):

- idempotent on the cover's approval reference (`reserve-cover-return:<approvalRef>`): the same reference and amount returns the first result, another amount is `idempotency-key-reused`;
- checks the cover exists, was paid, and the return does not exceed what it paid minus what has already come back (`cover-return-invalid`, `cover-return-exceeds-cover`);
- posts `debit LOANS_RECEIVABLE(loan), credit LOSS_RESERVE` (a new entry; the cover's own entry is untouched), puts the cents back in the fund counters (`FundState.returnCover`: balance up, paid out down, the invariant `balance = donated - paid out` holds), records `reserve.cover_return` (append-only) and `cover_request.returned_cents`, publishes `CoverReturned`, and appends a governance audit entry `RESERVE_COVER_RETURNED`;
- lock order is the cover row, then the fund row, as for a payout.

`committed()` for the member's yearly cap counts `amount - returned_cents`, because the member did not end up with money that went back. `IntegrationTest`-level proof is `CoverReturnIT`, which reproduces the race: the checker's signature and the member's payment commit in one transaction, so the payment lands before the event is delivered.

## Alternatives considered
- **Keep the loan and refund the member by hand.** Rejected: a live loan keeps a schedule, autopay and a record that count money nobody kept.
- **Reuse `CANCELLED` for the void loan.** Rejected: `CANCELLED` is a status the reserve cover code and the web treat as "cancelled by the member"; a distinct status says why and lets consumers drop the loan on purpose.
- **A new application status after a return.** Rejected: `APPROVED` plus `payoutIssue` is the state the desk already reads after a failed payout; one more status would change the contract, the DB check and every consumer for no new behaviour.
- **Edit the disbursement entry or the loan's transactions.** Rejected: ledger entries, schedule versions and loan transactions are append-only.
- **Hand the unused cover back by letting lending post it.** Rejected: lending has no ledger collaborator in this path on purpose (ADR 0020) and the fund counters are reserve's.
- **Treat the fund's share of a voided loan as the member's overpayment.** Rejected: it would let the member ask the program for the fund's money.

## Consequences
- A returned disbursement no longer needs an officer to unwind it: one transaction leaves the books balanced, the loan closed and the application ready for the next decision. The officer's remaining choice is send again or reject, and the reason is on the application.
- The member's payments on a void loan are refunded through PayPal after the commit; if PayPal cannot refund (not enough refundable captures) the money stays held on the void loan and the desk sees it (`overpaid_cents` on a `DISBURSEMENT_RETURNED` loan), never silently kept.
- Existing tests were not changed. `DisbursementPayoutSagaTest` still passes because without the unwind collaborator (a unit test with no ledger) the old flag behaviour remains as the fallback.
- A new `lending -> governance` dependency (audit only). Governance imports nothing from lending, so there is no cycle.

## Risks
- The member's refund depends on PayPal having captures that cover it; the failure is logged and visible on the void loan, but it needs a person.
- A void loan still shows its schedule on the desk's `GET /desk/loans/{id}/schedule` (history, all unpaid); the status says it is void.
- The existing 48-hour cooling-off cancel (`cancelWithinCoolingOff`) posts the money back to the ledger but leaves the loan row `ACTIVE`; it is a separate gap in the same family that this ADR does not change.
- `GET /loans/{id}` still answers for the owning member on a void loan so the refund endpoint keeps working; the Home list hides it.

## Follow-up: what the other modules do with `LoanVoided`
Closes the "Needed from other modules" rows for `desk-query`, `engagement` and `hardship`, and the cooling-off gap named under Risks. Every listener is an `@ApplicationModuleListener` and idempotent; no new module dependency was needed (each already depended on `lending`'s events).

- **`desk-query`** (`V3__voided_loans.sql`). The loan row becomes `DISBURSEMENT_RETURNED` (`CANCELLED` for a cooling-off cancel): outstanding 0, no next payment, no lateness, no autopay, and final, since later money, bucket and mode facts are ignored for it. It leaves the aging buckets, the on-time rate and the inclusion figures (`status not in ('CANCELLED','DISBURSEMENT_RETURNED')`); `byStatus` still lists it with 0 outstanding, and `status=DISBURSEMENT_RETURNED` is an accepted filter. `LoanApplicationChanged` carries no payout issue, so the application keeps its snapshot status (`APPROVED`) and a new `application_void` row records the return; `DeskApplicationRow.payoutIssue = RETURNED_AFTER_DISBURSEMENT` is derived on read while the status is `APPROVED`, so it does not depend on which event arrives first and disappears when the application is paid again or rejected. A projection reset of applications clears the table.
- **`engagement`** (`V5__void_loan_messages.sql`). Queued messages for the loan become `CANCELLED`; one message of type `LOAN_VOIDED` (in-app, plus SMS with consent and quiet hours) says the money did not reach the member's PayPal account, they owe nothing, there are no fees, and we will try again once the account is fixed (plus "the $X you paid is coming back" from `refundDueCents`). Idempotent on `loan:<loanId>:voided`. Once that message exists, any other message for the loan that arrives late is dropped.
- **`hardship`**. An open case on the loan closes as `SUPERSEDED` with reason `LOAN_VOIDED` through the normal close (hold, open-case flag, timers and open approval released), so no offer, reminder or decision follows. A void loan is not `ACTIVE`, so no new case opens.
- **Cooling-off cancel (confirmed bug, fixed).** `cancelWithinCoolingOff` posted its ledger entry but left the loan `ACTIVE` with its schedule, timers, retries and autopay; `LoanVoidFollowUpIT` failed with `expected CANCELLED, was ACTIVE` before the fix. It now calls `DisbursementReturn.closeCancelled` in the same transaction: the loan closes as `CANCELLED` (an existing status, so the contract enum is unchanged), timers and planned retries go, the mandate sync is requested, and `LoanVoided` is published with reason `COOLING_OFF_CANCELLED` and zero refund and cover, so the record, the desk, the reserve exposure, reminders and hardship drop the loan the same way. The member gets a one-line confirmation instead of the "payout came back" text, and no `payoutIssue` is set. `LoanRecalculator` and `integrity` treat a `CANCELLED` loan like a voided one (nothing derived from its schedule).

Residual risks: the cancel's ledger entry still credits the full requested amount, so a member who paid in before cancelling would push the receivable negative (no refund path exists for `CANCELLED` loans, unlike `DISBURSEMENT_RETURNED`); a payment that reaches a `CANCELLED` loan is recorded as a normal receipt. `DisbursementReturn.unwind` stamps the application with `clock.now()` while the rest of the service uses `Instant.now()`; they are the same in production, but with a mocked business clock the desk's newest-snapshot guard can ignore later application snapshots.

Tests: `LoanVoidFollowUpIT`, `VoidedLoanProjectionTest`, `LoanMessagesTest`, `MemberMessageTriggersLoanVoidedTest`, `CaseReactionsLoanVoidedTest`.

## Closing pass
Closes the residual risks of the follow-up above and two items of ADR 0026. Every item is resolved in code.

**1. A refund PayPal cannot make is kept and retried (`lending/V8__held_refunds.sql`).** The refund of money held for a member on a loan that never stood used to be a log line when PayPal could not make it. It is now a row in `lending.held_refund`, written by `HeldRefunds.owe` in the transaction that holds the money (the void, the cancel, a late payment), keyed by a reference that is also the idempotency key of the refund: `void-refund:<loanId>`, `cancel-refund:<loanId>` and `void-refund:<transactionId>` for one late payment. The same reference is never owed twice, and the payments module turns it into the `PayPal-Request-Id`.
- `HeldRefunds.attempt` runs in its own transaction, locks the row, and asks PayPal while it holds that lock. The PayPal call and the `REFUNDED` mark commit together, so a refund that happened is never repeated and one that did not stays `PENDING`. A failure is counted in a second transaction and the next attempt is scheduled with a doubling delay (`seed.lending.held-refund.retry-delay`, default `PT15M`, at most a day).
- The first attempt is the `LoanVoided` / `VoidedLoanPaymentHeld` listener in `CollectionEffects`, after the commit. The retry is `HeldRefundRetryJob` (`seed.jobs.held-refund-retry-ms`, worker role, off in tests with the other jobs), which calls `HeldRefunds.retryDue`.
- After `seed.lending.held-refund.max-attempts` (default 6) the row is `FAILED`, the money stays held and visible on the loan (`overpaid_cents`), and one governance audit entry `HELD_REFUND_FAILED` (subject the loan, actor `SYSTEM/lending`, reason a code) tells the desk. No event is published and engagement sends nothing: nothing changed for the member.
- Never more than is held: the refund is asked only while the loan still holds at least its amount. If the member already took the money through the refund endpoint (nothing held) the row closes as settled with no PayPal call; if only part is held (a payment was reversed) the row is `FAILED` with reason `HELD_LESS_THAN_REFUND` for a person to settle, because asking for a different amount under the same key would be a different request.

**2. The cooling-off cancel refunds what the member paid.** `closeCancelled` now posts the same settlement as the unwind (key `loan-cooling-off:<applicationId>`): debit program cash `P`, credit the receivable `O` (what the schedule still owed), the member's held overpayment `m` and the loss reserve `c`. The receivable therefore ends at exactly zero whoever paid what (before, it credited the whole `P` and went negative for a member who had paid). It works for an `ACTIVE` or `REPAID` loan. What the member had paid is owed as a `cancel-refund:` row and refunded by the mechanism above; what the fund had paid goes back to the fund through `LoanVoided.reserveCoverCents`, which the reserve already handles. A `CANCELLED` loan is now treated like a voided one when money reaches it: a payment is held and refunded, and a reversal or a refund lowers the held amount (`LoanRow.isUnwound`). The member's one-line confirmation does not yet say a refund is coming; that text belongs to engagement.

**3. Consent with a submission.** The fair-check consent has to exist before the application is checked, because eligibility and the fair check read the income that the consent allows PayPal to supply; recording it after acceptance would mean checking the application on income no consent covers. So the consent stays recorded first and a refused application takes it back: `submitApplication` notes whether the member already had a consent, and when the transaction fails it calls `UnderwritingFacade.revokeConsent` only if this request created the consent. The list shows it ended, and the income read for it is forgotten through the existing `ConsentRevoked` handling. A consent the member gave earlier is never touched, a failure to take the consent back never hides the refusal, and with the seeded income source nothing was recorded outside the transaction, so nothing needs taking back. `previewApplication` now says what it does when PayPal cannot be read: it answers from the stored income and sets `incomeStale = true` (`LoanApplicationPreview.incomeStale` in the contract; optional, always sent).

**4. One clock.** `MicroloanApplicationService` stamps every application change with the injected business clock (`now()`), as `DisbursementReturn` does, so the desk's newest-snapshot guard no longer sees snapshots from two clocks. A business clock that stands still (the test clock is noon of the business date) gives several snapshots the same instant; the desk guard accepts equal instants and the tests pass with it, but ordering within one tick is then the order of delivery. Production instants differ. `LoanVoidFollowUpIT` now proves the re-disbursement the old clock made impossible: return, officer pays again, new payout reference (`<id>-a2`), a new loan under `loan-<id>-disb-v1`, and the desk shows the new loan `ACTIVE`, the old one `DISBURSEMENT_RETURNED` and the application `DISBURSED` with no payout issue.

**Settings.** `seed.lending.held-refund.max-attempts` (`SEED_LENDING_HELD_REFUND_MAX_ATTEMPTS`, 6), `seed.lending.held-refund.retry-delay` (`SEED_LENDING_HELD_REFUND_RETRY_DELAY`, `PT15M`), `seed.jobs.held-refund-retry-ms` (`SEED_JOBS_HELD_REFUND_RETRY_MS`, 60000).

**Tests.** `HeldRefundIT` (first attempt fails, the job retries and refunds once; attempts exhausted reach `FAILED` with the audit entry and no member message; a reversed payment never lets the refund exceed what is held; a refund the member took themselves is not made twice; paid then cancelled, repaid in full then cancelled, a failing cancel refund retried, a late payment on a cancelled loan), `ApplicationConsentOnRefusalIT` (refused application takes back its consent and income, an earlier consent survives, the preview flag), `LoanVoidFollowUpIT` (re-disbursement), `CoolingOffCancelUnwindTest`, `SubmitConsentAndClockTest`, `HeldRefundsBackoffTest`.

**Residual risks.** The automatic refund of an overpaid `REPAID` loan (`RepaymentReceived`) still logs when PayPal has too little refundable; it is a different loan state and was out of scope. The refund row is attempted while its row lock is held across the PayPal call, so a very slow PayPal holds one refund row, never a loan lock.
